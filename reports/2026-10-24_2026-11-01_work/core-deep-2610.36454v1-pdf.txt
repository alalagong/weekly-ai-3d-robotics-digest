SVIn+: Multi-Modal Framework for Robust Underwater State
Estimation using 3D Sonar, Visual-Inertial, Water-Pressure, and DVL
Chinmay Burgul1, Xiaoming Zhao 2, Yewei Huang2, Michalis Chatzispyrou 1,
Marios Xanthidis 3, Ioannis Rekleitis 1, Alberto Quattrini Li 2
Abstract— As expansion and scalability in underwater oper-
ations becomes more important and challenging, autonomous
underwater vehicles (AUVs) become more relevant and the
necessity for reliable autonomy more critical. A fundamental
challenge in any autonomous underwater operation, from
control to safe navigation and mapping, is state estimation; a
notorious hard problem to address, especially in low visibility
conditions. In this paper, we tackle this problem, by presenting
SVIn+, a multi-sensor framework for robust state estimation of
AUVs equipped with an IMU, cameras, a water-pressure sensor,
a Doppler Velocity Log (DVL), and a compact 3D sonar. This
framework extends an established open-source state-of-the-art
visual-inertial SLAM, SVIn, by integrating water-pressure and
DVL measurements into a tightly-coupled nonlinear optimiza-
tion formulation resulting in improved estimates. To leverage
the structural information of recently available 3D acoustic
sonars while maintaining computational efficiency, we introduce
a hierarchical architecture that incorporates estimates from
a decoupled front-end via KISS-ICP, treated as relative pose
factors within the factor graph. The proposed framework is
validated through field deployments in a shipwreck and coral
reef, demonstrating drift mitigation and global consistency. To
support further research in underwater multi-modal fusion,
the code and the datasets can be found on project web-

I. INTRODUCTION
Reliable autonomy in underwater robotics needs robust
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) in per-
ceptually degraded GNSS-denied environments [1], ranging
from confined shipwrecks to dynamic coral reefs. These un-
derwater environments are characterized by many perceptual
challenges, including light attenuation and limited visibility.
Despite the plethora of SLAM approaches in the literature,
robust state estimation in underwater domains remains an
open problem. Traditional Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO)
is prone to catastrophic failure due to feature tracking loss
in turbid waters or motion-blur artifacts during high-dynamic
maneuvers [2]. Integrating water-pressure sensors (e.g., [3])
and Doppler Velocity Logs (DVLs) (e.g., [4]–[6]) can mit-
igate water-column depth and velocity drift; however, these

state estimation framework that achieves robust state esti-

work fuses inertial, camera vision, DVL, and water column
depth from water-pressure sensor measurements into the
factor graph. To provide structural consistency and mitigate
trajectory drift, we integrate relative pose factors derived
from a KISS-ICP front-end, which processes the point cloud
from a recently released 3D acoustic sonar. This decoupled

II. RELATEDWORK
Robust localization and mapping in underwater environ-
ments remains a major challenge due to the absence of
GNSS signals forcing underwater vehicles to rely heavily
on onboard sensing and dead reckoning, which leads to drift
accumulation over time [10]. Furthermore, optical sensing
is degraded by weak illumination, backscatter and turbidity
[11], while acoustic sensing suffers from multipath effects
[12] and limited resolution [13]. As a result, multi-sensor
integration is essential for achieving reliable, long-duration
underwater SLAM.
Early approaches fuse Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU),
Doppler Velocity Logs (DVL), depth, and acoustic position-
ing measurements within an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)
framework to reduce and manage uncertainties from hetero-
geneous sensors [10]. While effective at managing short-term
uncertainty, these systems lacked the dense environmental
context needed to constrain long-term drift.
To achieve higher local accuracy, visual-inertial odome-
try (VIO) emerged as a dominant paradigm. Early vision-
based underwater localization and mapping methods [14] are
primarily offline and rely on structure-from-motion (SfM)
and bundle adjustment. Subsequently, research shifted toward
online, loosely coupled SLAM solutions. [15] introduce a
loosely coupled SLAM framework for underwater shipwreck
inspection using a bag-of-words place recognition module
and factor graph optimization. [16] investigated the feasi-
bility of performing underwater SLAM using ORB-SLAM
[17]. [5], [18], [19] are loosely coupled systems that integrate
DVL with a visual–inertial SLAM framework.
More recently, researchers have moved toward tightly
coupled visual–inertial systems for higher localization ac-
curacy and estimator consistency. [4] adopts a Multi-State
Constraint Kalman Filter (MSCKF)-based SLAM frame-
work for tightly coupled visual–DVL multi-sensor fusion.
[20], [21] use cameras as the primary sensing modality to
achieve low-cost and dense reconstruction. Similarly, [22]
focuses on mitigating perception degradation through multi-
sensor fusion. AquaSLAM [6] proposes an online SLAM
framework that jointly estimates the camera–DVL extrinsic
transformation within the state estimation process. However,
vision-based approaches remain fundamentally limited by
restricted field of view, light attenuation, backscatter, and
turbidity, and therefore typically operate only in relatively
clear and well-illuminated environments.
To address these challenges, researchers have also adopted
sonar as a primary perception modality. Sonar-based, vision-
free SLAM methods [23]–[27] typically construct 2D maps
under planar motion assumptions and are constrained by
relatively low spatial resolution compared to optical sensing.
There are also methods using a mechanical sonar [28] or
dual sonars [29]–[31] for 3D mapping. SVIn [28] implements
a tightly coupled hybrid framework based on OKVIS [32].
While the incorporation of acoustic sensing improves robust-
ness, the relatively low spatial resolution and low update
frequency of the sonar measurements limit their ability to
support dense reconstruction.
With the recent development of compact 3D sonar sensors,
lightweight AUV systems can acquire denser point clouds
at relatively higher update rates. There emerges hybrid ap-
proaches that combine the 3D sonar with the visual-inertial
SLAM pipeline [7]–[9]. [9] presents a loosely coupled fusion
of visual and 3D sonar measurements for long-distance
cave mapping. [7] adopts a loosely coupled framework
that integrates 3D sonar with an inertial navigation system
(INS) composed of a DVL, Attitude Heading Reference Sys-
tem(AHRS), and water-pressure sensor for pose estimation
and mapping. In contrast, [8] proposes a tightly coupled
visual–inertial–sonar SLAM framework without DVL inte-
gration, and the system is evaluated only in a relatively small-
scale (6m×7m) environment.
Overall, tightly coupled multi-sensor fusion state-
estimation framework for robots equipped with 3D sonar
remains an open problem. A reliable and robust long-term
multi-sensor state estimation solution that enables dense and
accurate reconstruction in underwater environments, partic-
ularly in murky water, remains a challenge. In this paper,
we address this gap by proposing a partially tightly coupled
visual, sonar, DVL, water pressure framework that jointly
optimizes measurements from multiple modalities including
sonar, camera, water-pressure and DVL to address this gap.

V. EXPERIMENTALVALIDATION
To evaluate the performance and robustness of the pro-
posed SVIn+, we collected a series of datasets in diverse
and challenging underwater environments off the coast of
Barbados. Our experiments aim to demonstrate that the
integration of 3D sonar constraints into a tightly-coupled
visual-inertial-DVL estimator mitigates trajectory drift by
leveraging acoustic structural context. In the following sub-
sections, we detail our hardware configuration and extrinsic
calibration procedures, describe the characteristics of our
shipwreck and coral reef datasets, and provide a quantitative
comparison against state-of-the-art baselines.
Fig. 3: A detailed view of the onboard sensor configuration.
A. Experimental Setup
We utilize the Aqua2 AUV from independent robotics
equipped with a MicroStrain 3DM-CV7 AHRS, two AR0234
global shutter cameras in stereo configuration with hardware
synchronization, a Water Linked DVL-A50 Doppler Velocity
Log, a Bar30 pressure sensor, and a Water Linked 3D-15
sonar (Fig. 3).
Accurately knowing the extrinsic transformation across
this multi-sensor suite is critical for robust state estimation.
The transformation between Camera{C}and IMU{I}
reference frames is calibrated using Kalibr [35]. The rigid
extrinsic transformations from IMU to DVL{V}and Water-
Pressure Sensor{D}frames are provided by the manu-
facturer of the Aqua2 robot. Finally, the relative rotation
and translation between 3D Sonar{L}and Camera{C}
are determined following the approach in [9]. We solve
the Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem using 3D-2D corre-
spondences extracted from four cinder block targets placed
underwater and visible in both the sonar point clouds and vi-
sual images. All experiments are conducted on a workstation
running Ubuntu 24.04, equipped with an Intel Core Ultra 7
155H CPU, 64 GB of RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 5000
Ada Generation GPU. The pipeline was also successfully
evaluated in real-time on a low-power laptop (Intel Core
i7-10510U CPU, 16 GB of RAM, NVIDIA GTX 1050),
demonstrating its suitability for deployment on resource-
constrained AUV hardware.
B. Dataset Description
We collected three datasets in two distinct locations off
the coast of Barbados using the Aqua2 platform. To the
best of our knowledge, there are currently no publicly
available underwater datasets that provide a synchronized
suite comprising stereo vision, DVL, and 3D sonar data
in such complex environments. These sequences encompass
a range of perceptual challenges, from confined structural
interiors to expansive open-water reefs:
•BajanQueen-01(Shipwreck Interior): This sequence
consists of a moderate-distance trajectory∼75 meters
over about 8 minutes, within the confined, structured
PAGE 5
environment of a shipwreck. Both missions start and
end at a fixed ArUco marker, visited a total of 3 times,
providing a reliable reference to evaluate start-to-end
translation and rotation drift.
•BajanQueen-02(Shipwreck): A longer,∼600-meter
trajectory over around 55 minutes, which covers the
entire shipwreck, testing robustness against significant
depth variations and sparse visual features.
•Folkestone-01(Shallow Reef): conducted in a natural
unstructured environment, the trajectory spans∼280
meters over 1252 seconds, challenging the vision fron-
tend with dynamic lighting despite a relatively flat
trajectory.
C. Evaluation of Return-to-Origin Trajectory
We evaluate the accumulated trajectory drift using
BajanQueen-01sequence. Since global ground truth (e.g.,
GPS or long-baseline acoustic positioning) is unavailable
in these unstructured underwater environments, we rely
on a “return-to-origin” metric. We compare our proposed
SVIn+framework against two baselines:SVIn2visual-
inertial framework and KISS-ICP as(Sonar Odom). Global
loop closures were disabled to isolate the performance of
the underlying continuous odometry, ensuring that compar-
isons were not confounded by global correction mechanisms.
In addition to the online baselines, we utilize an offline
Structure-from-Motion (SfM) pipeline [36]. We performed
an extensive parameter tuning to get the best results from
each method for fair comparison.
Fig. 4 shows the trajectories over a complete start-to-
end loop, with the initial and final positions denoted by a
circle and a star, respectively. While Sonar Odom provides
geometric context, it exhibited significantly higher drift due
to the low-frequency nature of acoustic updates and noise
(e.g., coming from multipath effects) in confined shipwreck
compartments. The offline SfM reference (Colmap) shows
noticeable discontinuities where visual tracking failed due to
environmental conditions.
SVIn+ overall maintains a consistent path throughout the
dataset, where the start and end are close to each other, com-
pared to SVIn2. As shown qualitatively in Fig. 9, theSVIn+
trajectory results in the ArUco marker board being closer
compared to SVIn2 baselines. Quantifying the accumulated
drift via the ArUco marker board reveals that the proposed
SVIn+achieves a return-to-origin error of just 0.52 m,
representing a substantial improvement over the 0.81 m error
yielded bySVIn2. This lower drift is further evidenced by
the resulting point cloud from SVIn+, which clearly shows
three corridors on the left side of the shipwreck and the
room on the right side (Fig. 5). These results highlight
the effectiveness of the proposed multi-sensor fusion in
bounding global drift and providing a coherent structural
reconstruction.

E. Ablation study
To evaluate the individual contribution of each sensor
modality, we performed an incremental ablation study. First,
we performed an evaluation with the Aqua2 going over a
shallow area at fixed depth and following a lawnmower
pattern for qualitative assessment. Fig. 8 compares the es-
timated trajectories of baseline SVIn2 with DVL and Depth
from water pressure sensor integration. The incorporation
of DVL measurements effectively constrains drift in the
x−yplane, while depth integration bounds verticalz-
axis error. The tightly-coupled fusion of both the sensors

VI. LESSONSLEARNED ANDFUTUREWORK
The stability and drift reduction achieved bySVIn+estab-
lish a robust foundation for several future research directions:
Robustness to sensor degeneracy: While the current factor
graph manages uncertainty, extreme conditions can render
specific modalities completely unreliable (e.g., sparse 3D
sonar registration failures, as depicted in Figure 12). We plan
to develop an adaptive switching mechanism for proactive
failure detection and system resilience.
Fig. 11: Comparison of (a) the 3D feature point cloud generated by the visual

3D sonar point scans (purple) from the BajanQueen-01 dataset.
Global mapping and loop closure: To transition from con-
tinuous odometry to a complete SLAM system for long-
endurance missions, we will integrate multi-modal global
place recognition. Leveraging both visual and acoustic sig-
natures will ensure reliable re-localization and correct accu-
mulated error.
Online spatial calibration: Because unmodeled dynamic
alignment variations (such as DVL misalignment) introduce
systematic errors that offline calibration cannot fully resolve,
we plan to implement an online extrinsic calibration backend
to dynamically maximize trajectory accuracy.
VII. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we presented SVIn+, a robust multi-modal
framework for underwater state estimation that integrates
measurements from 3D acoustic sonar, visual-inertial sen-
sors, a water-pressure sensor and a Doppler Velocity Log
(DVL). To overcome the perceptual degradation common
in underwater environments, such as light attenuation and
turbidity, our tightly-coupled nonlinear optimization frame-
work jointly optimizes these heterogeneous measurements
within a local sliding window. To efficiently leverage the
structural context of 3D sonars without compromising real-
