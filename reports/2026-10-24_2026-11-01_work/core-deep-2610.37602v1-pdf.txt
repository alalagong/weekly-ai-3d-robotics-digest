RESPLE2: Inertial-Informed Recursive Non-Uniform Spline
Estimation for Multi-Sensor Odometry with Global Refinement
Ziyu Cao, William Talbot, Song Li, Pakpong Chirarattananon, Fredrik Gustafsson, Marco Hutter, and Kailai Li
Abstract—We present RESPLE2, a recursive multi-sensor state
estimation framework using non-uniform B-splines for 6-DoF
motion representation. By exploiting the knot-clamping bound-
ary property, we propose a theoretically grounded mechanism
for exact control-point prediction directly informed by inertial
kinematics along the trajectory, with knot placement adapted
to motion and measurement availability. Within an iterated
extended Kalman filter, we perform this prediction using a subset
of inertial measurements. A subsequent reset step preserves
trajectory consistency as the knots extend. The update step
further incorporates heterogeneous sensor measurements at their
native timestamps through continuous-time observation models,
supporting flexible combinations of LiDARs, cameras, inertial
measurement units, and leg kinematics. We further develop a
lightweight global optimization module that directly refines non-
uniform B-spline control points by incorporating loop-closure
constraints derived from LiDAR-visual cues. We conduct ex-
tensive evaluations on public and experimental datasets across
handheld, tracked, legged, and hybrid aerial-legged platforms,
covering highly dynamic maneuvers with extreme accelerations.
Compared with state-of-the-art multi-sensor odometry systems,
RESPLE2 achieves competitive or superior estimation accu-
racy and robustness in the corresponding sensor configurations,
particularly under aggressive motion, while maintaining real-
time operation. We publicly release our code and experimental
datasets athttps://github.com/ASIG-X/RESPLE2.

equipped with multimodal sensors [1], [2], have been devel-
oped for demanding applications spanning search and rescue,
logistics, healthcare, entertainment, household assistance, in
situ forestry, and environmental monitoring [3]–[7].

new modalities. Furthermore, they have been validated pri-

and complex terrain interaction [2], [15].
Conventional multi-sensor estimation methods represent dy-
namic motion in discrete time and use recursive filtering
or nonlinear sliding-window optimization, with motion states
associated with specific timestamps at fixed frame rates.
During online estimation, asynchronous sensor measurements
are typically approximated, e.g., through temporal interpola-
tion [9], [16]. This paradigm often scales poorly as sensor rates
and the number of sensors vary, particularly when changing
sensors without synchronized triggering. Moreover, measure-
ments from many exteroceptive sensors, such as LiDARs and
rolling-shutter cameras, exhibit motion distortion during agile
maneuvers and require explicit modeling or compensation for
undistortion in state estimation. These design choices can
compromise the spatiotemporal fidelity of raw observations
and alter underlying noise characteristics, potentially degrad-
ing estimation accuracy and robustness while increasing both
methodological and engineering complexity [10], [17].

ber and temporal placement of estimates from observations,
facilitating the integration of asynchronous multi-sensor read-

design. Piecewise-linear models show promise for LiDAR-
only odometry, e.g., Traj-LO [18], due to simplicity and
computational efficiency, but may inadequately capture non-
linear motion during rapid or aggressive maneuvers. A more
advanced, well-studied approach usesexactly sparsetempo-
ral Gaussian processes (GPs), in which system dynamics
are modeled as a Markovian LTV-SDE process, such as

Most existing online continuous-time estimators solve max-
imum likelihood estimation (MLE) or maximum a posteriori
(MAP) estimation problems in a sliding-window fashion [17],
[24], [26], [27]. Although these optimization-based methods
generally achieve high accuracy and improve robustness to
sensor degeneracy, their real-time deployment is challenging
and often requires carefully designed solvers, which compli-
cates the integration of additional sensor modalities.
Recursive Bayesian estimators, such as the extended
Kalman filter (EKF), offer a methodologically lightweight
alternative; however, they remain scarce and underexplored
in continuous-time multi-sensor odometry systems and are
restricted to a narrow, fixed set of sensor configurations.
For example, CTE-MLO performs recursive estimation using
a GP-based continuous-time representation for LiDAR-only
odometry [21]. RESPLE is the first6-DoF B-spline-based
recursive motion estimator, supporting both LiDAR-only and
LiDAR-inertial odometry [25]. But its uniform knot spacing
imposes a globally fixed temporal knot interval, where ac-
curate control-point estimation requires knot intervals to be
short enough for the polynomial to capture aggressive motions,
yet long enough to include sufficient sensor measurements to
constrain all degrees of freedom at each update, increasing
susceptibility to geometric degeneracy. It is therefore theorized
that well-chosen, non-uniform knots can bring both accuracy
and computational benefits [26], [28]. Despite preliminary
efforts to develop non-uniform B-spline LVIO using sliding-
window optimization [26], the broader methodological and
practical potential of this motion-adaptive representation re-
mains underexplored.
More fundamentally, despite its growing adoption in prac-
tical systems, the theoretical formulation of B-spline-based
odometry systems remains incomplete. Regardless of whether
estimation uses filtering or sliding-window optimization, or
whether knot spacing is uniform or non-uniform, B-spline
control points do not lie on the motion trajectory in general.
Consequently, existing theory has not established exact kine-
matic models for the states (i.e., the control points) analogous
to those used in discrete-time or GP-based continuous-time
approaches. As a result, new control points have not been
predicted or initialized along the trajectory by directly inte-
grating kinematic information, such as IMU measurements.
Furthermore, no existing non-uniform B-spline-based odome-
try system natively supports loop closure for global refinement.
To address these methodological and engineering limi-
tations, we present RESPLE2, a novel recursive Bayesian
estimation framework that represents continuous-time 6-DoF
motion using non-uniform B-splines within an iterated ex-
tended Kalman filter (IEKF). Unlike existing B-spline-based
estimators, our framework introduces a inertial-informed pro-
cess model to guarantee exact prediction of control points
located on the motion trajectory. Moreover, RESPLE2 enables
a versatile estimation framework supporting a configurable
fusion of LiDAR, camera, IMU, and leg joint measurements.
Extensive real-world validation spans generic mobile platforms
and, particularly, emerging robotic embodiments. More specif-
ically, our key contributions are summarized below.

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Multi-Sensor Odometry
Popular multi-sensor odometry systems remain predomi-
nantly based on discrete-time formulations, in which tightly
coupled filtering-based or optimization-based estimators can
fuse disparate sensor modalities more consistently and accu-
rately than their loosely coupled counterparts [14], [29]. The
most closely related system, FAST-LIVO2 [30], uses IMU
strapdown integration within an error-state iterated Kalman
filter (ESIKF) andVoxelMapfor local map management [31].
The sequential updates adopt LiDAR point-to-plane residuals
using planes extracted from the local map and photometric
visual residuals computed by warping image patches stored
in the map and efficiently retrieved through on-demand voxel
raycasting. Point-LIO focuses on robust LIO under dynamic
motion and IMU saturation, with point-wise updates for both
inertial and LiDAR measurements [32]. For legged robots,
kinematic information from joint encoders and contact sensing
provides an additional measurement modality to complement
inertial and exteroceptive sensors. Leg-KILO [13] fuses Li-
DAR, inertial, and leg joint measurements using an error-state
Kalman filter (ESKF) for quadrupedal odometry, with point-
wise updates for individual sensors as in Point-LIO [32].
The proposed RESPLE2 adoptsVoxelMap[31] for local
map management and uses feature-based visual residuals.
These design choices build on the state of the art and place less
emphasis on variations in map representation while prioritiz-
ing estimation robustness and versatile multi-sensor designs,
enabling LVIO, LKIO, and beyond.
B. Continuous-Time Motion Estimation
We refer readers to [22], [28] for comprehensive surveys and
comparisons of continuous-time motion estimation methods
and their relative advantages in robotics. GP-based methods
provide the most theoretically complete alternative, enabling
constant-time interpolation of the mean and covariance be-
tween state variables and supporting online MAP estimation
through sliding-window optimization. Following this strat-
egy, systems fusing inertial measurements with individual
modalities, including camera, LiDAR, radar, ultra-wideband,
and GNSS, have been reported [20], [22], [28], [33]. CTE-
MLO [21] further combines the GP representation with an
EKF for LiDAR-only odometry. Its filtering-based design
makes it a GP-based counterpart to RESPLE2. However, to
the best of our knowledge, no LVIO or LKVIO systems using
the GP representation have been proposed.
Recent improvements in interpolation efficiency have pro-
moted the use of B-splines in multi-sensor odometry [34], [35].
As with other continuous-time methods, almost all B-spline-
based systems perform MAP estimation by optimizing control
points over one or more spline segments [28]. For example,
CLINS, SLICT2, and CLIC [24], [36], [37] use sliding-
window optimization to estimate uniform B-spline trajectories
for LIO or LVIO. RESPLE [25] was the first to introduce
recursive B-spline estimation using an IEKF, supporting LO
and LIO within a unified design. This lightweight estima-
tor achieves comparable or superior accuracy and greater
robustness under dynamic motion than these optimization-
based systems. Its LIO variant provides a direct baseline for
assessing RESPLE2’s exact control-point prediction within a
non-uniform B-spline framework.
For online non-uniform B-spline estimation, the knot can be
placed by using the maximum magnitudes of IMU-measured
angular velocity and acceleration as heuristic indicators of
motion complexity, selecting shorter segments when these
values are high [26]. As a non-uniform B-spline LVIO system,
Coco-LIC [26] uses sliding-window MAP estimation and
determines knot locations by subdividing a uniform knot distri-
bution based on IMU excitation. Alternatively, the number and
local distribution of visual keyframes can guide knot density
selection [38]. A further methodological challenge is that the
blending matrix needed for interpolation is no longer constant
but depends on future knots to be determined in advance. This
is problematic for Coco-LIC [26] as the approach must wait
for future IMU measurements to determine knot placements,
inducing optimization delays [39]. LIO-Mars [39] sets future
knots according to an expected interval, correcting these knots
when future LiDAR scans arrive.
For both uniform and non-uniform B-splines, no existing
online estimation scheme enablesexactinitialization or pre-
diction of new control points by directly integrating kinematic
information, such as IMU measurements, along the motion tra-
jectory. For example, RESPLE [25] uses a process model with
a constant transition matrix to predict new control points by
carrying forward the velocities at previous knots. SLICT2 [24]
initializes new control points at the trajectory endpoint plus
an offset obtained by integrating IMU measurements, causing
the resulting B-spline to deviate from the predicted trajec-
tory. Coco-LIC [26] adopts a simple scheme that copies
the most recent control points. This theoretical limitation
can compromise estimation performance under challenging

conditions, such as aggressive dynamics, particularly with ex-
teroceptive degradation. In comparison, RESPLE2 exploits the
knot-clamping boundary property of non-uniform B-splines to
ensure that the latest control point always lies on the trajectory,
thereby providing a theoretical guarantee for exact control-
point prediction using kinematic models informed by inertial
measurements, with knot placement guided by both kinematic
and exteroceptive cues. This enables significantly more accu-
rate control-point prediction without estimation delays.
Furthermore, native support for loop closure and global
refinement within B-spline estimation remains limited. For
loop closure detection, BTC [40] uses a binary and triangle
descriptor to encode global and local point-cloud structure for
3D place recognition. Its extension, iBTC [41], incorporates
visual information, outperforming geometry-based BTC and
bag of visual words in LVIO systems while remaining efficient
for online operation. CLINS [36] adopts a two-stage global
correction scheme at loop closures for uniform B-splines
in LIO: pose-graph optimization of discrete-time key-scan
poses using loop-closure constraints, followed by control-
point updates that align the continuous-time trajectory while
preserving its local shape. RESPLE2 integrates iBTC [41]
for loop closure detection in the LVIO setting and is the
first to provide native global trajectory refinement by directly
optimizing non-uniform B-spline control points.

in [25] for6-DoF continuous-time LiDAR odometry, where
an iterated update step was proposed to handle point cloud
association during the measurement update. The TriS model,
however, has not yet been extended to non-uniform B-splines
for multi-sensor continuous-time motion estimation. More
importantly, the transition matrixA k in (7) typically prop-
agates the RCPs by extrapolating motion from preceding knot
intervals, without incorporating current kinematic information.
Such propagation can yield inaccurate trajectory priors dur-
ing rapid changes in motion, compromising data association
particularly in perceptually degenerate scenes where weak
observational constraints limit subsequent correction.
IV. SYSTEMOVERVIEW
The proposed RESPLE2 framework is illustrated in Fig. 2
in a multi-sensor setting for dynamic egomotion estimation.
The basic configuration comprises an IMU and a LiDAR,
with optional camera and legged-kinematic measurements
(joint positions and velocities). Incoming LiDAR scans are
downsampled using voxel-grid filtering, while IMU samples
are split by alternating assignment into twodisjointsubsets
for prediction and measurement update, respectively, over
the current knot interval. When camera images are available,
they are undistorted, and visual features are tracked across
frames using a Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi (KLT) sparse optical-
flow tracker [9]. When available, leg joint measurements are
preprocessed using forward kinematics to obtain foot positions
and velocities with respect to the robot base frame.
We introduce a generalized spline-state-space model for
the non-uniform B-splines, where a new process model is
proposed based on the knot-clamping condition that guaran-
tees control point initialization directly informed by inertial
measurements on the motion trajectory (Section V). Once the
criteria for creating a new knot are met, we clamp a new knot
to the spline endpoint and initialize its associated control point
through exact RCP prediction using the IMU measurement
subset (Section VI-A). The knot at the previous endpoint
is then unclamped to accommodate incoming measurements,
for which a reset step is first performed to correct RCPs
over the finalized knot spacing together with their covariance
(Section VI-B). Afterwards, IMU measurements assigned to
the update and other sensor measurements falling within
the current knot interval are buffered and fused through the
iterated update (Section VI-C) via kinematic interpolation to
construct residuals according to the modality-specific observa-
tion models (Section V-C). This includes establishing point-
to-plane correspondences between LiDAR points and the local
voxel map [31]. After convergence, the voxel map is updated
with the undistorted LiDAR points, and new image features
are initialized with depth from the co-registered LiDAR points.
The estimated control points from odometry are sent to the
global refinement module that maintains the global spline tra-
jectory and map. Loop closures are detected using iBTC [41],
after which global refinement is performed directly on the non-
uniform B-spline control points to correct accumulated drift
while preserving local kinematic consistency (Section VII).

B. RCP Reset for Unclamping
Unclamping:Introducing a new knot att n during prediction
shifts the clamped boundary fromt n−1 tot n, where (10)
applies. The resultingunclampingatt n−1 changes the local

VIII. EVALUATION
A. Implementation
We implement RESPLE2 as a ROS2 package with separate
nodes for multi-sensor odometry and global spline refinement.
We use a minimal set of dependencies, including Eigen for
numerical computations and OpenMP to parallelize B-spline
kinematic interpolation, LiDAR point processing, nearest-
neighbor association, and linearization during global optimiza-
tion. Each node is typically allocated five OpenMP threads.
We use Pinocchio for computing leg forward kinematics [49].
The global refinement solver is implemented from scratch and
solves the sparse linear systems using Eigen’s CHOLMOD
interface, reusing symbolic factorization across iterations.
We combine the sensor models in Section V-C to enable
LiDAR-inertial (LIO), LiDAR-visual-inertial (LVIO), LiDAR-
leg-inertial (LKIO), and LiDAR-leg-visual-inertial (LKVIO)
odometry within the same filtering scheme, where multi-modal
observations in each knot interval are stacked into a single
measurement vector. Additionally, following the system design
of RESPLE2, we provide a LiDAR-only variant (RESPLE2-
LO) to support platforms without an IMU. Here, the original
inertial-informed process model is replaced by a constant-
velocity model with the same knot-clamping boundary prop-
erty, predicting position using the linear velocity estimate
and scaling the latest orientation increment by the ratio of
successive knot intervals. Accordingly, the reset preserves
the position, orientation and linear velocity at the previous
endpoint while imposing zero acceleration.

B. Runtime and Computational Analysis
Tab. IX reports the per-frame runtime breakdown on three
sequences, one for each operating mode:flip1(LIO),

LiDAR frame, respectively. The IEKF update is the dominant
stage throughout, accounting for roughly one third of the per-

in LKIO, followed by the image preprocessing, V oxelMap
maintenance and LiDAR preprocessing. The proposed RCP
prediction and reset are lightweight, together requiring at most
0.51 msper frame and less than7%of the total runtime on
every sequence. The LVIO mode incurs the highest cost, as
visual processing adds about4.1 msper frame and the visual
measurements further enlarge the IEKF update.

X. CONCLUSION
We presented RESPLE2, a recursive continuous-time6-DoF
state estimation framework using non-uniform B-splines. By
exploiting the knot-clamping boundary condition, the frame-
work directly predicts control points from IMU-integrated
PAGE 16
16
kinematics on the trajectory and adapts knot placement to mo-
tion and measurement availability. A subsequent control point
reset preserves local trajectory consistency as the spline is
extended. In addition, a lightweight global optimization mod-
ule is developed to directly refine the non-uniform B-spline
control points by combining loop-closure constraints, reduc-
ing accumulated global drift and preserving local kinematic
consistency. RESPLE2 supports flexible combinations of Li-
DAR, visual, inertial, and leg-kinematic measurements, as well
as LiDAR-only operation. Extensive real-world evaluations
across diverse platforms demonstrate accuracy and robustness
comparable to or exceeding those of state-of-the-art meth-
ods while maintaining real-time operation, with particularly
strong performance under aggressive motion and severe IMU
saturation. These results highlight the potential of motion-
adaptive, recursive B-spline estimation for robust multi-sensor
egomotion estimation under challenging conditions. For future
work, we plan to investigate adaptive sensor weighting and
visual feature constraints that do not depend on LiDAR-
derived depth to further improve estimation robustness under
sensor degeneracy. Another promising direction is to integrate
spline-based state estimation directly with optimization- or
learning-based motion planning onboard mobile robots.
