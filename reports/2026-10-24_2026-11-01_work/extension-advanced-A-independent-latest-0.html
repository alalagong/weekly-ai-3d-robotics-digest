Since such relationships rarely hold exactly in practice, prior distributions should encourage them while at the same time retaining support for scientifically plausible departures. Accounting for these relationships becomes more pressing in modern regression, where high-dimensionality is usually present and regularisation is essential. $R^2$ decomposition priors provide an interpretable foundation for regularisation by placing a prior on total explained variance and distributing the implied signal across model terms. We introduce the Proximal R2 prior, which uses proximal relaxations to encode these relationships as soft constraints on the variance allocation. Notably, these results demonstrate that the structural constraints in the model do not hinder posterior recovery.
