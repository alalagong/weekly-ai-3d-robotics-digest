Recognizing the approach to that decision requires relating motor intent to distal response and the… ▽ More Flexible robotic endoscopes provide access through tortuous anatomy, where friction, deformation, and wall interaction separate commanded insertion from useful distal progress. Operators interpret this mismatch and retreat to recover a navigable configuration. Recognizing the approach to that decision requires relating motor intent to distal response and the local visual scene. We introduce Multimodal Progress-Residual Fusion (MPRF), a causal model that anticipates operator retreat from commands, electromagnetic (EM) tracking, and endoscopic video. A nominal-progress model predicts the response expected in normal or successful navigation; a temporal predictor combines its EM residual with all three observed modalities. In a 150-episode colon-phantom collection, MPRF achieves 0.580 +/- 0.007 AUPRC across three seeds, compared with 0.511 for the strongest reduced-modality control. Complete multimodal histories provide the principal ranking advantage, while the residual adds an explicit intent-response representation with a small, uncertain increment over raw fusion. Analysis of frozen alarms relates warning timing to the behavioral label and explains the increase in event recall under a longer matching window. These results establish a basis for behavioral assistance during operator-controlled navigation. △ Less

    Accuracy Is Not Enough: An Efficiency- and Statistics-Aware Benchmark of ResNet50 and Vision Transformer for Explainable Multi-Class Gastrointestinal Diseases Classification

    Authors: Akwasi Asare

    Abstract: Automated classification of gastrointestinal (GI) findings in endoscopic images could support early diagnosis, but architectures are often chosen on accuracy alone. We present a controlled, reproducible benchmark of ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 for eight-class GI classification on the public Kvasir dataset (4,000 images), using a stratified split (400 test images)… ▽ More Automated classification of gastrointestinal (GI) findings in endoscopic images could support early diagnosis, but architectures are often chosen on accuracy alone. We present a controlled, reproducible benchmark of ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 for eight-class GI classification on the public Kvasir dataset (4,000 images), using a stratified split (400 test images) and stratified group 5-fold cross-validation over all images. In cross-validation, ResNet50 and ViT-B/16 reached 92.12% and 91.70% accuracy, and a two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure showed equivalence within a +/-3 percentage-point margin (difference +0.43 points, 90% CI -0.33 to +1.18; p < 0.001). The architectures differed mainly in cost: ResNet50 had 3.6 times fewer parameters and 4.3 times fewer operations, 2.5-fold lower single-image CPU latency (69 vs. 177 ms) and 3.8-fold higher GPU throughput. Cross-validated calibration was similar (expected calibration error 0.021 vs. 0.023). An edge-oriented transformer, MobileViT-S, was most accurate on the test split (95.5%) but lost 9.4 points when an acquisition artefact present in some classes (the ScopeGuide inset) was occluded, whereas ResNet50 was not significantly affected. On 500 unseen polyp images with expert masks, both models' Grad-CAM maps focused on the lesion (3.6 times chance). On a de-duplicated external subset of GastroVision (1,998 images), accuracy fell to 78-80% for all three models and differences between them remained small. Accuracy differences between architectures were thus small relative to the effects of shortcut learning and domain shift, whereas efficiency differences were large. An efficient CNN is a defensible default for resource-constrained screening, but no model is deployment-ready without local validation. △ Less
