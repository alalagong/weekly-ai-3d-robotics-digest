    Multi-level Constrained Learning for Underwater Image Enhancement

    Authors: Wangtao Bao, Liping Xie, Kanjian Zhang, Huimin Lu

    Abstract: Underwater image enhancement aims to correct color and recover detail degraded by wavelength-dependent attenuation, scattering, and noise. Paired-reference supervision constrains output similarity, but does not by itself specify the color… ▽ More Underwater image enhancement aims to correct color and recover detail degraded by wavelength-dependent attenuation, scattering, and noise. Paired-reference supervision constrains output similarity, but does not by itself specify the color mapping, the strength of local detail corrections, or consistency with the degraded observation. We propose MCL-UIE, a paired-supervised framework that addresses these requirements by constraining restoration operations and the training objective. Monotonic Chromatic Transform (MCT) restricts global color mapping in log-LMS space through positive diagonal entries and bounded cross-channel coefficients. Uncertainty-Gated Wavelet Reconstruction (UGWR) modulates high-frequency residuals with a learned confidence gate calibrated against normalized wavelet reconstruction errors. During training, Reference-Anchored Degradation Replay (RADR) renders the enhanced image and its paired reference using shared latent degradation proxies, encouraging both to reproduce the input. The three components share a restoration network that combines structured color correction, locally controlled detail recovery, and reference-anchored replay. MCL-UIE ranks first in thirteen of sixteen full-reference dataset-metric comparisons and produces competitive visual results on no-reference benchmarks. Objective ablations indicate that confidence calibration is most effective when combined with replay supervision. RADR is used only during training and adds no inference-time computation. Our code is available at https://github.com/Wangtao-Bao/MCL-UIE. △ Less

    SVIn+: Multi-Modal Framework for Robust Underwater State Estimation using 3D Sonar, Visual-Inertial, Water-Pressure, and DVL

    Authors: Chinmay Burgul, Xiaoming Zhao, Yewei Huang, Michalis Chatzispyrou, Marios Xanthidis, Ioannis Rekleitis, Alberto Quattrini Li

    Abstract: As expansion and scalability in underwater oper- ations becomes more important and challenging, autonomous… ▽ More As expansion and scalability in underwater oper- ations becomes more important and challenging, autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) become more relevant and the necessity for reliable autonomy more critical. A fundamental challenge in any autonomous underwater operation, from control to safe navigation and mapping, is state estimation; a notorious hard problem to address, especially in low visibility conditions. In this paper, we tackle this problem, by presenting SVIn+, a multi-sensor framework for robust state estimation of AUVs equipped with an IMU, cameras, a water-pressure sensor, a Doppler Velocity Log (DVL), and a compact 3D sonar. This framework extends an established open-source state-of-the-art visual-inertial SLAM, SVIn, by integrating water-pressure and DVL measurements into a tightly-coupled nonlinear optimiza- tion formulation resulting in improved estimates. To leverage the structural information of recently available 3D acoustic sonars while maintaining computational efficiency, we introduce a hierarchical architecture that incorporates estimates from a decoupled front-end via KISS-ICP, treated as relative pose factors within the factor graph. The proposed framework is validated through field deployments in a shipwreck and coral reef, demonstrating drift mitigation and global consistency.     Abstract: …results in natural domains, but suffers from limited generative fidelity and weak alignment with user-provided layouts when applied to degraded scenes (i.e., low-light, underwater). We primarily attribute these limitations to the "contextual illusion dilemma" in degraded conditions, where foreground instances are overwhelmed by context-dominant frequ… ▽ More Layout-to-image (L2I) generation has exhibited promising results in natural domains, but suffers from limited generative fidelity and weak alignment with user-provided layouts when applied to degraded scenes (i.e., low-light, underwater). We primarily attribute these limitations to the "contextual illusion dilemma" in degraded conditions, where foreground instances are overwhelmed by context-dominant frequency distributions. Motivated by this, our paper proposes a new Frequency-Inspired Contextual Disentanglement Generative (FICGen) paradigm, which seeks to transfer frequency knowledge of degraded images into the latent diffusion space, thereby facilitating the rendering of degraded instances and their surroundings via contextual frequency-aware guidance. To be specific, FICGen consists of two major steps. Firstly, we introduce a learnable dual-query mechanism, each paired with a dedicated frequency resampler, to extract contextual frequency prototypes from pre-collected degraded exemplars in the training set. Secondly, a visual-frequency enhanced attention is employed to inject frequency prototypes into the degraded generation process. To alleviate the contextual illusion and attribute leakage, an instance coherence map is developed to regulate latent-space disentanglement between individual instances and their surroundings, coupled with an adaptive spatial-frequency aggregation module to reconstruct spatial-frequency mixed degraded representations. Extensive experiments on 5 benchmarks involving a variety of degraded scenarios-from severe low-light to mild blur-demonstrate that FICGen consistently surpasses existing L2I methods in terms of generative fidelity, alignment and downstream auxiliary trainability. △ Less
