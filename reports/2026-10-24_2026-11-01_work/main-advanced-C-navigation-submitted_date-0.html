Although semantically capable, such action-centric training does not explicitly model how the agent's visual observations evolve under its predicted motion. Generative world-action models (WAMs) jointly predict future observations and actions, yet existing WAMs for continuous VLN do not condition joint future-view and action generation on geometry-aware representations inferred from the observation history. We propose RPKD (Random Prototype Knowledge Distillation), which discards this assumption by comparing both networks against a shared bank of randomly initialized, frozen prototype vectors. Alignment is therefore carried out in an external reference space rather than in the teacher's native feature coordinates: the teacher side is projected by fixed random maps that carry no trainable parameters, and only the student adapts. Two branches handle the transfer: a logit branch aligning global prototype similarity distributions and a feature branch matching spatially-resolved prototype activation maps. Both operate within the same fixed prototype space, which can be interpreted as a random feature embedding that approximately preserves inter-sample similarity structure, so the prototype bank and the distillation objective stay unchanged across teacher-student pairs while only the input projections are instantiated per architecture. In low-category settings, decoupling the prototype vocabulary from the task label space yields richer supervision than the label space alone, an advantage absent from logit-based methods, whose supervisory signal collapses as class count shrinks. This advantage is especially relevant in real-world scenarios where the label space is typically constrained. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of RPKD, achieving a maximum gain of +7.98% over OFA on CIFAR-100, +12.44% on ImageNet-100, and +1.28% on ImageNet-1K.
