We propose RPKD (Random Prototype Knowledge Distillation), which discards this assumption by comparing both networks against a shared bank of randomly initialized, frozen prototype vectors. Alignment is therefore carried out in an external reference space rather than in the teacher's native feature coordinates: the teacher side is projected by fixed random maps that carry no trainable parameters, and only the student adapts. Two branches handle the transfer: a logit branch aligning global prototype similarity distributions and a feature branch matching spatially-resolved prototype activation maps. In low-category settings, decoupling the prototype vocabulary from the task label space yields richer supervision than the label space alone, an advantage absent from logit-based methods, whose supervisory signal collapses as class count shrinks. This advantage is especially relevant in real-world scenarios where the label space is typically constrained. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of RPKD, achieving a maximum gain of +7.98% over OFA on CIFAR-100, +12.44% on ImageNet-100, and +1.28% on ImageNet-1K.
